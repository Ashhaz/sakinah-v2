To feel better: do this

Don’t try everything. Pick one that feels doable, and do it now. Fixed numbers are given only where the hadith gives them; otherwise say it as often as you like.

Name three blessings and say Alḥamdulillāh

Before you move on, say out loud three specific things you have. Gratitude changes where your attention sits.

الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ

Alḥamdu lillāh.

All praise is for Allah.

“…and Alḥamdulillāh fills the scale.” (Abū Mālik al-Ashʿarī (may Allah be pleased with him))

Allah says: “If you are grateful, I will surely increase you” (14:7).

Sahih Muslim 223 Qur’an 14:7

Remember Allah with the four beloved phrases

Breathe slowly and say any of these quietly, as many times as feels right. There is no fixed number, so the counters are only a help.

سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ، وَالْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ، وَلَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ، وَاللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ

SubḥānAllāh, wal-ḥamdu lillāh, wa lā ilāha illallāh, wallāhu akbar.

Glory be to Allah; praise be to Allah; there is no god but Allah; Allah is the Greatest.

“The most beloved words to Allah are four: SubḥānAllāh, Alḥamdulillāh, Lā ilāha illallāh and Allāhu akbar. It does not matter which one you begin with.” (Samurah ibn Jundub (may Allah be pleased with him))

Gentle, practical steps

Small things you can try today. Not religious rulings, just ordinary wellbeing help that sits well alongside the guidance above.

  1. Follow the 20-20-20 habit: every 20 minutes, look at something about 20 feet (6 metres) away for 20 seconds.
  2. Blink fully and often when reading screens. Dry eyes are largely a blinking issue.
  3. Reduce screen brightness at night and put your screen slightly below eye level.
  4. Get your eyes tested. Headaches and strain are often fixed by a correct prescription.
  5. Close your eyes for a minute at the end of each prayer. Make it a micro-rest.

These are general wellbeing suggestions, not religious rulings or medical advice. If symptoms persist, please see a health professional.
