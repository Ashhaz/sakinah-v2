Burnout & Exhaustion

Tired even after resting, and motivation has gone

The “always improve yourself” mindset is exhausting. The Prophet ﷺ repeatedly taught moderation and warned against overburdening yourself, even in worship.

Start here

Rest is a right, not a reward. The Prophet ﷺ told us to ease off when tired.

If you only have a minute, read these. The full library follows.

And Allah wants to lighten for you [your difficulties]; and mankind was created weak.

Weakness is built into being human. Allah knows it and wants to lighten your load.

Qur’an 4:28 Sūrat An-Nisāʾ

The Prophet ﷺ saw a rope hanging between two pillars and asked, “What is this?” They said, “It is for Zaynab; when she gets tired she holds on to it.” He said, “Untie it. Let each of you pray while he is energetic, and when he becomes tired, let him rest.”

To feel better: do this

Don’t try everything. Pick one that feels doable, and do it now. Fixed numbers are given only where the hadith gives them; otherwise say it as often as you like.

Pour your heart out in sujūd

Pray two rakʿahs, or your next prayer, and make a lot of du’a in prostration. Tell Allah everything that is on your heart.

The closest a servant is to his Lord is when he is prostrating, so make abundant du’a. (Abū Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him))

Sahih Muslim 482

Remember Allah with the four beloved phrases

Breathe slowly and say any of these quietly, as many times as feels right. There is no fixed number, so the counters are only a help.

سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ، وَالْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ، وَلَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ، وَاللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ

SubḥānAllāh, wal-ḥamdu lillāh, wa lā ilāha illallāh, wallāhu akbar.

Glory be to Allah; praise be to Allah; there is no god but Allah; Allah is the Greatest.

“The most beloved words to Allah are four: SubḥānAllāh, Alḥamdulillāh, Lā ilāha illallāh and Allāhu akbar. It does not matter which one you begin with.” (Samurah ibn Jundub (may Allah be pleased with him))

Also: “Truly, in the remembrance of Allah do hearts find peace” (13:28), and the Prophet ﷺ said the one who remembers his Lord and the one who does not are like the living and the dead (Bukhari 6407).

Sahih Muslim 2137 Qur’an 13:28 Sahih al-Bukhari 6407

Lā ḥawla wa lā quwwata illā billāh

Say it whenever you feel you don’t have the strength. It means admitting that your strength comes from Allah.

لَا حَوْلَ وَلَا قُوَّةَ إِلَّا بِاللَّهِ

Lā ḥawla wa lā quwwata illā billāh.

There is no power and no strength except with Allah.

“Shall I not tell you of a treasure from the treasures of Paradise? It is: lā ḥawla wa lā quwwata illā billāh.” (Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī (may Allah be pleased with him))

Sahih al-Bukhari 6384 Sahih Muslim 2704

From the Prophet ﷺ

“The religion is ease, and no one overburdens himself in the religion except that it overcomes him. So be moderate, stay close to the mark, give glad tidings, and seek help in the morning, in the afternoon, and in part of the night.”

Narrated by Abū Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him)

Even about worship, the Prophet ﷺ warns against pushing past your limit.

Sahih al-Bukhari 39Ṣaḥīḥ

The Prophet ﷺ saw a rope hanging between two pillars and asked, “What is this?” They said, “It is for Zaynab; when she gets tired she holds on to it.” He said, “Untie it. Let each of you pray while he is energetic, and when he becomes tired, let him rest.”

Narrated by Anas ibn Mālik (may Allah be pleased with him)

A direct instruction to stop forcing yourself when you are exhausted.

To ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAmr, who was fasting every day and praying all night, the Prophet ﷺ said: “Do not do that. Fast and break the fast, pray and sleep. For your body has a right over you, your eyes have a right over you, and your family has a right over you.”

Narrated by ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAmr (may Allah be pleased with him)

Narrated by Abū Juḥayfah (may Allah be pleased with him)

Gentle, practical steps

Small things you can try today. Not religious rulings, just ordinary wellbeing help that sits well alongside the guidance above.

  1. Pick one thing to drop this week. Burnout is usually a signal that something has to go.
  2. Protect a daily non-productive window (a walk, a meal, nothing on a screen).
  3. Lower the bar on purpose: do the minimum version of your habits, not zero.
  4. If exhaustion has lasted weeks, speak to a doctor. Sleep, iron, thyroid, vitamin D and mood can all play a part.
