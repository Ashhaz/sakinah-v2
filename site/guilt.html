Guilt, Shame & Feeling Unforgivable

“I did something wrong” versus “I am something wrong”

Guilt says you made a mistake. Shame says you are the mistake. The door of tawbah is open for the first, and Allah answers the second by reminding you of the dignity He gave you.

Guilt that helps, and shame that harms

Guilt that moves you back to Allah and towards change is healthy. Shame tells you to hide and to believe you are worthless. The Qur’an says the opposite: Allah honoured the children of Adam (17:70) and tells those who have wronged themselves not to despair (39:53). Repentance is between you and Allah, and if you have wronged a person, make it right with them where you can.

To feel better: do this

Don’t try everything. Pick one that feels doable, and do it now. Fixed numbers are given only where the hadith gives them; otherwise say it as often as you like.

Ask forgiveness: istighfār

Say it slowly, with your heart present. The Prophet ﷺ said he did this more than seventy times a day. No fixed number is required of you, so the counter is just a help.

أَسْتَغْفِرُ اللَّهَ وَأَتُوبُ إِلَيْهِ

Astaghfirullāha wa atūbu ilayh.

I seek forgiveness from Allah and turn to Him in repentance.

“By Allah, I seek forgiveness from Allah and turn to Him in repentance more than seventy times a day.” (Abū Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him))

Sahih al-Bukhari 6307

Pour your heart out in sujūd

Pray two rakʿahs, or your next prayer, and make a lot of du’a in prostration. Tell Allah everything that is on your heart.

The closest a servant is to his Lord is when he is prostrating, so make abundant du’a. (Abū Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him))

Sahih Muslim 482

Raise your hands and ask

Raise your hands and ask Allah for what you need, in your own words. A single sentence is enough.

“Your Lord is Ever-Living and Generous. He is shy to turn away empty the hands of His servant when he raises them to Him.” (Salmān al-Fārisī (may Allah be pleased with him))

Sunan Abi Dawud 1488 Jami‘ at-Tirmidhi 3556Ḥasan (Tirmidhī); Ṣaḥīḥ (al-Albānī)

SubḥānAllāhi wa bi-ḥamdih

Say it a hundred times, for example after Fajr or before bed.

سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ وَبِحَمْدِهِ

SubḥānAllāhi wa bi-ḥamdih.

Glory be to Allah and praise be to Him.

“Whoever says “SubḥānAllāhi wa bi-ḥamdih” one hundred times a day, his sins will be forgiven even if they are like the foam of the sea.” (Abū Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him))

Gentle, practical steps

Small things you can try today. Not religious rulings, just ordinary wellbeing help that sits well alongside the guidance above.

  1. Say astaghfirullāh now. Don’t wait until you “feel ready”.
  2. Add a small good deed straight after: pray two rakʿahs, give a little charity, call someone.
  3. If you wronged a person, make it right: apologise, return what is theirs.
  4. Tell yourself the truth: “I made a mistake. I am not a mistake.”
  5. Where it’s a health matter (compulsive guilt, scrupulosity), speak to a counsellor.
