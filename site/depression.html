Depression & Heavy Sadness

When everything feels dark, grey and heavy

Feeling low, numb or empty does not mean your faith is broken. The Qur’an and the Prophet ﷺ speak about sorrow honestly and gently. You are allowed to feel this, and you are allowed to ask for help.

Faith and professional care walk together

Depression can be an illness as well as an emotional and spiritual struggle. The Prophet ﷺ said that Allah has not sent down a disease except that He also sent down its cure (Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 5678). Seeing a doctor or a therapist is part of seeking that cure, not a lack of trust in Allah.

To feel better: do this

Don’t try everything. Pick one that feels doable, and do it now. Fixed numbers are given only where the hadith gives them; otherwise say it as often as you like.

Pour your heart out in sujūd

Pray two rakʿahs, or your next prayer, and make a lot of du’a in prostration. Tell Allah everything that is on your heart.

The closest a servant is to his Lord is when he is prostrating, so make abundant du’a. (Abū Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him))

Sahih Muslim 482

Read a few verses slowly

Open the Qur’an, read a short passage with a translation, and let it be a conversation. Even five verses count.

…

O mankind, there has come to you instruction from your Lord and healing for what is in the breasts and guidance and mercy for the believers.

“Recite the Qur’an, for it will come on the Day of Resurrection as an intercessor for those who recite it.” (Abū Umāmah al-Bāhilī (may Allah be pleased with him))

Qur’an 10:57 Sahih Muslim 804

Lā ḥawla wa lā quwwata illā billāh

Say it whenever you feel you don’t have the strength. It means admitting that your strength comes from Allah.

لَا حَوْلَ وَلَا قُوَّةَ إِلَّا بِاللَّهِ

Lā ḥawla wa lā quwwata illā billāh.

There is no power and no strength except with Allah.

“Shall I not tell you of a treasure from the treasures of Paradise? It is: lā ḥawla wa lā quwwata illā billāh.” (Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī (may Allah be pleased with him))

Sahih al-Bukhari 6384 Sahih Muslim 2704

Do one small kindness today

Send a kind message, smile, check on someone, or help with something small. Doing good for others often lifts the heart.

“Do not belittle any good deed, even if it is meeting your brother with a cheerful face.” (Abū Dharr (may Allah be pleased with him))

And: Allah helps His servant as long as the servant is helping his brother (Muslim 2699).

Sahih Muslim 2626 Sahih Muslim 2699

From the Qur’an

…

For indeed, with hardship [will be] ease. Indeed, with hardship [will be] ease.

Not “after” the hardship is gone, but with it: ease is already travelling alongside the difficulty.

Qur’an 94:5–6 Sūrat Ash-Sharḥ
…

Your Lord has not taken leave of you, nor has He detested [you]. And the Hereafter is better for you than the first [life]. And your Lord is going to give you, and you will be satisfied.

These words were revealed to comfort the Prophet ﷺ himself during a period of anxious waiting. If they were needed by him, they are for us too.

Qur’an 93:3–5 Sūrat Aḍ-Ḍuḥā
…

Those who have believed, and whose hearts find peace in the remembrance of Allah. Truly, in the remembrance of Allah do hearts find peace.

Not a magic switch, but a place to return to again and again, even for one sentence at a time.

Qur’an 13:28 Sūrat Ar-Raʿd
…

He said, “I only complain of my suffering and my grief to Allah, and I know from Allah that which you do not know.”

The Prophet Yaʿqūb ﷺ wept for years over the loss of Yūsuf (see also 12:84). Grief is human. Complaining to Allah – not about Him – is allowed.

Qur’an 12:86 Sūrat Yūsuf
…

And [mention] the man of the fish, when he went off in anger and thought that We would not decree [anything] upon him; and he called out within the darknesses, “There is no deity except You; exalted are You. Indeed, I have been of the wrongdoers.” So We responded to him and saved him from the distress. And thus do We save the believers.

He called out from “the darknesses” and was answered. Your own dark place is not too dark for a du’a to reach out of it.

Qur’an 21:87–88 Sūrat Al-Anbiyāʾ
…

Say, “O My servants who have transgressed against themselves [by sinning], do not despair of the mercy of Allah. Indeed, Allah forgives all sins. Indeed, it is He who is the Forgiving, the Merciful.”

If your sadness is tangled with guilt or shame, this verse is a direct invitation to come back.

Qur’an 39:53 Sūrat Az-Zumar
…

Allah does not charge a soul except [with that within] its capacity. It will have [the consequence of] what [good] it has gained, and it will bear [the consequence of] what [evil] it has earned. “Our Lord, do not impose blame upon us if we have forgotten or erred. Our Lord, and lay not upon us a burden like that which You laid upon those before us. Our Lord, and burden us not with that which we have no ability to bear. And pardon us; and forgive us; and have mercy upon us. You are our Protector…”

If it feels unbearable, ask for relief: the verse itself teaches you the words.

Qur’an 2:286 Sūrat Al-Baqarah

✦

The Prophet ﷺ in his own “year of sorrow”

Historians of the Prophet’s ﷺ life describe a period in which he lost two of his closest supports, his wife Khadījah and his uncle Abū Ṭālib, around the same time. Then, when he travelled to Ṭāʾif seeking help and a hearing for his message, he was rejected and driven out.

ʿĀʾishah (may Allah be pleased with her) once asked him whether he had faced a day harder than Uḥud. He told her about that day at ʿAqabah, and said: “So I set off, overwhelmed with grief, and I did not recover until I reached Qarn ath-Thaʿālib.” The angel of the mountains then offered to crush the people of Makkah between two mountains, but he answered that he hoped Allah would bring from their descendants those who would worship Allah alone.

He felt the weight, was honest about it, kept going, and kept hope for others even in his lowest hour.

Sahih al-Bukhari 3231 Sahih Muslim 1795

The du’a for sorrow and anxiety

اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي عَبْدُكَ، ابْنُ عَبْدِكَ، ابْنُ أَمَتِكَ، نَاصِيَتِي بِيَدِكَ، مَاضٍ فِيَّ حُكْمُكَ، عَدْلٌ فِيَّ قَضَاؤُكَ، أَسْأَلُكَ بِكُلِّ اسْمٍ هُوَ لَكَ سَمَّيْتَ بِهِ نَفْسَكَ، أَوْ عَلَّمْتَهُ أَحَدًا مِنْ خَلْقِكَ، أَوْ أَنْزَلْتَهُ فِي كِتَابِكَ، أَوِ اسْتَأْثَرْتَ بِهِ فِي عِلْمِ الْغَيْبِ عِنْدَكَ، أَنْ تَجْعَلَ الْقُرْآنَ رَبِيعَ قَلْبِي، وَنُورَ صَدْرِي، وَجَلَاءَ حُزْنِي، وَذَهَابَ هَمِّي

Allāhumma innī ʿabduka, ibnu ʿabdika, ibnu amatik. Nāṣiyatī biyadik, māḍin fiyya ḥukmuk, ʿadlun fiyya qaḍāʾuk. Asʾaluka bikulli ismin huwa lak, sammayta bihi nafsak, aw ʿallamtahu aḥadan min khalqik, aw anzaltahu fī kitābik, awistaʾtharta bihi fī ʿilmil-ghaybi ʿindak, an tajʿalal-Qurʾāna rabīʿa qalbī, wa nūra ṣadrī, wa jalāʾa ḥuznī, wa dhahāba hammī.

O Allah, I am Your servant, the son of Your servant, the son of Your maidservant. My forelock is in Your hand; Your command over me is carried out; Your decree concerning me is just. I ask You by every name that is Yours — by which You named Yourself, or taught to any of Your creation, or revealed in Your Book, or kept to Yourself in the knowledge of the unseen — to make the Qur’an the spring of my heart, the light of my chest, the departure of my sorrow and the release of my worry.

The Prophet ﷺ said that whoever is afflicted by worry or grief and says this, Allah will remove his worry and grief and replace it with relief. The Companions asked if they should learn it; he said yes, whoever hears it should learn it.

Musnad Aḥmad (ḥadīth of Ibn Masʿūd) – graded Ṣaḥīḥ by Ibn Ḥibbān and al-Albānī, Silsilah aṣ-Ṣaḥīḥah no. 199

For worry, grief, weakness and debt

اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنَ الْهَمِّ وَالْحَزَنِ، وَالْعَجْزِ وَالْكَسَلِ، وَالْبُخْلِ وَالْجُبْنِ، وَضَلَعِ الدَّيْنِ وَغَلَبَةِ الرِّجَالِ

Allāhumma innī aʿūdhu bika minal-hammi wal-ḥazan, wal-ʿajzi wal-kasal, wal-bukhli wal-jubn, wa ḍalaʿid-dayni wa ghalabatir-rijāl.

O Allah, I seek refuge in You from worry and grief, from weakness and laziness, from stinginess and cowardice, from being overwhelmed by debt and from being overpowered by people.

Reported by Anas (may Allah be pleased with him) – the Prophet ﷺ used to say it often.

Sahih al-Bukhari 6369Ṣaḥīḥ

The du’a of Yūnus from the darkness

لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا أَنْتَ سُبْحَانَكَ إِنِّي كُنْتُ مِنَ الظَّالِمِينَ

Lā ilāha illā anta, subḥānaka, innī kuntu minaẓ-ẓālimīn.

There is no god but You; glory be to You. Truly, I have been among the wrongdoers.

The Prophet ﷺ said that no Muslim supplicates his Lord with these words for anything, except that Allah answers him (Tirmidhī 3505, from Saʿd ibn Abī Waqqāṣ).

Qur’an 21:87 Jami‘ at-Tirmidhi 3505Ṣaḥīḥ (al-Albānī)

Gentle, practical steps

Small things you can try today. Not religious rulings, just ordinary wellbeing help that sits well alongside the guidance above.

  1. Tell one real person today, even in one sentence: “I haven’t been okay lately.”
  2. Do the smallest next thing: drink water, open the window, step outside for five minutes of daylight.
  3. Pray where you are, even if it is short and feels empty. Showing up counts.
  4. If low mood has lasted two weeks or more, please see a doctor or therapist. This is a health matter, not a failure.

If it feels like too much right now

If you are thinking about ending your life or harming yourself, or you are in danger, please contact your local emergency number or a crisis line now, and tell someone near you. You deserve support, and reaching out is a strength.

  • India: Tele-MANAS, 14416 (toll-free; also 1800-891-4416)
  • USA: call or text 988
  • UK & Ireland: Samaritans, 116 123
  • Anywhere: findahelpline.com lists services by country

Numbers can change, so please check one is current for your country. Allah says: “…and do not kill yourselves. Indeed, Allah is ever Merciful to you” (Qur’an 4:29, part of the verse). You can also speak to a trusted imam, teacher or family member, and to a doctor or counsellor.
