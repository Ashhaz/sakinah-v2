Du’as for hard moments

Words you can say when you have none of your own, and also in your own language.

You can always speak to Allah in your own words. These are words taught by Allah and His Messenger ﷺ. Arabic Qur’an text is loaded live from a Qur’an API; the other Arabic is short and well-known, and each du’a lists its source.

For worry, grief, weakness and debt

اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي أَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنَ الْهَمِّ وَالْحَزَنِ، وَالْعَجْزِ وَالْكَسَلِ، وَالْبُخْلِ وَالْجُبْنِ، وَضَلَعِ الدَّيْنِ وَغَلَبَةِ الرِّجَالِ

Allāhumma innī aʿūdhu bika minal-hammi wal-ḥazan, wal-ʿajzi wal-kasal, wal-bukhli wal-jubn, wa ḍalaʿid-dayni wa ghalabatir-rijāl.

O Allah, I seek refuge in You from worry and grief, from weakness and laziness, from stinginess and cowardice, from being overwhelmed by debt and from being overpowered by people.

Reported by Anas (may Allah be pleased with him) – the Prophet ﷺ used to say it often.

Used on: Depression & Heavy Sadness · Stress & Anxiety · Financial Uncertainty · Burnout & Exhaustion · Scrolling, Focus & Procrastination

Sahih al-Bukhari 6369Ṣaḥīḥ

The du’a for sorrow and anxiety

اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي عَبْدُكَ، ابْنُ عَبْدِكَ، ابْنُ أَمَتِكَ، نَاصِيَتِي بِيَدِكَ، مَاضٍ فِيَّ حُكْمُكَ، عَدْلٌ فِيَّ قَضَاؤُكَ، أَسْأَلُكَ بِكُلِّ اسْمٍ هُوَ لَكَ سَمَّيْتَ بِهِ نَفْسَكَ، أَوْ عَلَّمْتَهُ أَحَدًا مِنْ خَلْقِكَ، أَوْ أَنْزَلْتَهُ فِي كِتَابِكَ، أَوِ اسْتَأْثَرْتَ بِهِ فِي عِلْمِ الْغَيْبِ عِنْدَكَ، أَنْ تَجْعَلَ الْقُرْآنَ رَبِيعَ قَلْبِي، وَنُورَ صَدْرِي، وَجَلَاءَ حُزْنِي، وَذَهَابَ هَمِّي

Allāhumma innī ʿabduka, ibnu ʿabdika, ibnu amatik. Nāṣiyatī biyadik, māḍin fiyya ḥukmuk, ʿadlun fiyya qaḍāʾuk. Asʾaluka bikulli ismin huwa lak, sammayta bihi nafsak, aw ʿallamtahu aḥadan min khalqik, aw anzaltahu fī kitābik, awistaʾtharta bihi fī ʿilmil-ghaybi ʿindak, an tajʿalal-Qurʾāna rabīʿa qalbī, wa nūra ṣadrī, wa jalāʾa ḥuznī, wa dhahāba hammī.

O Allah, I am Your servant, the son of Your servant, the son of Your maidservant. My forelock is in Your hand; Your command over me is carried out; Your decree concerning me is just. I ask You by every name that is Yours — by which You named Yourself, or taught to any of Your creation, or revealed in Your Book, or kept to Yourself in the knowledge of the unseen — to make the Qur’an the spring of my heart, the light of my chest, the departure of my sorrow and the release of my worry.

The Prophet ﷺ said that whoever is afflicted by worry or grief and says this, Allah will remove his worry and grief and replace it with relief. The Companions asked if they should learn it; he said yes, whoever hears it should learn it.

Used on: Depression & Heavy Sadness · Heartbreak, Rejection & Betrayal · Hopelessness: “What’s the Point?”

Musnad Aḥmad (ḥadīth of Ibn Masʿūd) – graded Ṣaḥīḥ by Ibn Ḥibbān and al-Albānī, Silsilah aṣ-Ṣaḥīḥah no. 199

The du’a of distress

لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ الْعَظِيمُ الْحَلِيمُ، لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ رَبُّ الْعَرْشِ الْعَظِيمِ، لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ رَبُّ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَرَبُّ الْأَرْضِ وَرَبُّ الْعَرْشِ الْكَرِيمِ

Lā ilāha illallāhul-ʿAẓīmul-Ḥalīm, lā ilāha illallāhu Rabbul-ʿarshil-ʿaẓīm, lā ilāha illallāhu Rabbus-samāwāti wa Rabbul-arḍi wa Rabbul-ʿarshil-karīm.

There is no god but Allah, the Mighty, the Forbearing. There is no god but Allah, Lord of the Mighty Throne. There is no god but Allah, Lord of the heavens, Lord of the earth and Lord of the Noble Throne.

Reported by Ibn ʿAbbās (may Allah be pleased with them both): the Prophet ﷺ used to say this at times of distress.

Used on: Depression & Heavy Sadness · Stress & Anxiety · Overwhelmed by News & Online Arguments · Grief & Losing Someone · Hopelessness: “What’s the Point?”

Sahih al-Bukhari 6346 Sahih Muslim 2730Ṣaḥīḥ

The du’a of Yūnus from the darkness

لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا أَنْتَ سُبْحَانَكَ إِنِّي كُنْتُ مِنَ الظَّالِمِينَ

Lā ilāha illā anta, subḥānaka, innī kuntu minaẓ-ẓālimīn.

There is no god but You; glory be to You. Truly, I have been among the wrongdoers.

The Prophet ﷺ said that no Muslim supplicates his Lord with these words for anything, except that Allah answers him (Tirmidhī 3505, from Saʿd ibn Abī Waqqāṣ).

Used on: Depression & Heavy Sadness · Guilt, Shame & Feeling Unforgivable · Hopelessness: “What’s the Point?”

Qur’an 21:87 Jami‘ at-Tirmidhi 3505Ṣaḥīḥ (al-Albānī)

“Allah is enough for us”

حَسْبُنَا اللَّهُ وَنِعْمَ الْوَكِيلُ

Ḥasbunallāhu wa niʿmal-wakīl.

Allah is sufficient for us, and He is the best disposer of affairs.

Ibn ʿAbbās reported that Ibrāhīm said it when he was thrown into the fire, and Muḥammad ﷺ said it when people warned: “The people have gathered against you.”

Used on: Stress & Anxiety · Fear of the Future · Overwhelmed by News & Online Arguments

Qur’an 9:129

When you catch yourself saying “if only…”

قَدَرُ اللَّهِ وَمَا شَاءَ فَعَلَ

Qadarullāhi wa mā shāʾa faʿal.

Allah has decreed, and what He willed He did.

The Prophet ﷺ taught to say this instead of “if only I had…”, because “if only” opens the door to the whispers of Satan.

Used on: Fear of Falling Behind · Fear of the Future · Heartbreak, Rejection & Betrayal

Qur’an 20:114

For halal provision and independence from others

اللَّهُمَّ اكْفِنِي بِحَلَالِكَ عَنْ حَرَامِكَ، وَأَغْنِنِي بِفَضْلِكَ عَمَّنْ سِوَاكَ

Allāhummak-finī biḥalālika ʿan ḥarāmik, wa aghninī bifaḍlika ʿamman siwāk.

O Allah, suffice me with what You have made lawful against what You have made unlawful, and make me independent of everyone besides You through Your bounty.

Taught by ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib (may Allah be pleased with him) as something the Prophet ﷺ taught him.

Sahih al-Bukhari 6312Ṣaḥīḥ

Istikhārah – asking Allah for the best choice

After praying two voluntary rakʿahs, say (meaning): “O Allah, I seek Your guidance through Your knowledge and ask for ability through Your power, and I ask You of Your great bounty. You are able and I am not; You know and I do not; You are the Knower of the unseen. O Allah, if You know that this matter is good for me in my religion, my livelihood and the outcome of my affairs, then decree it for me, make it easy for me, and bless me in it. And if You know that this matter is bad for me in my religion, my livelihood and the outcome of my affairs, then turn it away from me and turn me away from it, and decree for me the good wherever it may be, and make me content with it.” (One mentions the matter by name at “this matter”.)

Meaning given in summary; for the exact Arabic wording please read the full hadith (Jābir, may Allah be pleased with him – the Prophet ﷺ taught it to us as he taught us a sūrah of the Qur’an).

Used on: Fear of the Future · Heartbreak, Rejection & Betrayal

Sahih al-Bukhari 1162Ṣaḥīḥ

When a calamity or loss strikes

إِنَّا لِلَّهِ وَإِنَّا إِلَيْهِ رَاجِعُونَ، اللَّهُمَّ أْجُرْنِي فِي مُصِيبَتِي وَأَخْلِفْ لِي خَيْرًا مِنْهَا

Innā lillāhi wa innā ilayhi rājiʿūn. Allāhumma ʾjurnī fī muṣībatī wa akhlif lī khayran minhā.

Indeed we belong to Allah, and indeed to Him we will return. O Allah, reward me in my calamity and replace it for me with something better.

Reported by Umm Salamah (may Allah be pleased with her) from the Prophet ﷺ.

Used on: Grief & Losing Someone

Qur’an 7:200 Sahih al-Bukhari 3282 Sahih Muslim 2610Ṣaḥīḥ

Sayyid al-Istighfār – the master supplication for forgiveness

اللَّهُمَّ أَنْتَ رَبِّي لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا أَنْتَ، خَلَقْتَنِي وَأَنَا عَبْدُكَ، وَأَنَا عَلَى عَهْدِكَ وَوَعْدِكَ مَا اسْتَطَعْتُ، أَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنْ شَرِّ مَا صَنَعْتُ، أَبُوءُ لَكَ بِنِعْمَتِكَ عَلَيَّ، وَأَبُوءُ بِذَنْبِي فَاغْفِرْ لِي، فَإِنَّهُ لَا يَغْفِرُ الذُّنُوبَ إِلَّا أَنْتَ

Allāhumma anta rabbī lā ilāha illā ant. Khalaqtanī wa anā ʿabduk, wa anā ʿalā ʿahdika wa waʿdika mastaṭaʿt. Aʿūdhu bika min sharri mā ṣanaʿt. Abūʾu laka biniʿmatika ʿalayya, wa abūʾu bidhanbī faghfir lī, fa-innahu lā yaghfirudh-dhunūba illā ant.

O Allah, You are my Lord; there is no god but You. You created me and I am Your servant, and I keep Your covenant and promise as best I can. I seek refuge in You from the evil of what I have done. I acknowledge Your favour upon me and I acknowledge my sin, so forgive me, for none forgives sins except You.

Reported by Shaddād ibn Aws (may Allah be pleased with him).

Used on: Guilt, Shame & Feeling Unforgivable · A Habit You Can’t Seem to Break
