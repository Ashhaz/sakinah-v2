Grief & Losing Someone

When someone you love is gone and life keeps going

Grief has no deadline, and crying is not weakness. The Prophet ﷺ cried at a funeral and lost children of his own. Here is what Allah and His Messenger ﷺ say to the grieving.

You don’t have to carry it alone

Grief can feel like depression and sometimes becomes it. If months pass and you cannot function, or you have dark thoughts, please talk to a doctor or counsellor as well as to people who loved them. See also the page on depression.

To feel better: do this

Don’t try everything. Pick one that feels doable, and do it now. Fixed numbers are given only where the hadith gives them; otherwise say it as often as you like.

Pour your heart out in sujūd

Pray two rakʿahs, or your next prayer, and make a lot of du’a in prostration. Tell Allah everything that is on your heart.

The closest a servant is to his Lord is when he is prostrating, so make abundant du’a. (Abū Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him))

Sahih Muslim 482

Read a few verses slowly

Open the Qur’an, read a short passage with a translation, and let it be a conversation. Even five verses count.

…

O mankind, there has come to you instruction from your Lord and healing for what is in the breasts and guidance and mercy for the believers.

“Recite the Qur’an, for it will come on the Day of Resurrection as an intercessor for those who recite it.” (Abū Umāmah al-Bāhilī (may Allah be pleased with him))

Qur’an 10:57 Sahih Muslim 804

Lā ḥawla wa lā quwwata illā billāh

Say it whenever you feel you don’t have the strength. It means admitting that your strength comes from Allah.

لَا حَوْلَ وَلَا قُوَّةَ إِلَّا بِاللَّهِ

Lā ḥawla wa lā quwwata illā billāh.

There is no power and no strength except with Allah.

“Shall I not tell you of a treasure from the treasures of Paradise? It is: lā ḥawla wa lā quwwata illā billāh.” (Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī (may Allah be pleased with him))

Sahih al-Bukhari 6384 Sahih Muslim 2704

From the Prophet ﷺ

When his son Ibrāhīm died, the Prophet ﷺ wept and said: “The eyes shed tears and the heart grieves, and we say only what pleases our Lord. O Ibrāhīm, we are truly grieved by your departure.”

Narrated by Anas ibn Mālik (may Allah be pleased with him)

Sahih al-Bukhari 1303 Sahih Muslim 2315Ṣaḥīḥ

The Prophet ﷺ said: “No Muslim is afflicted by a calamity and says what Allah has commanded: ‘Indeed we belong to Allah, and to Him we return. O Allah, reward me in my calamity and replace it with something better,’ except that Allah will replace it with something better for him.”

Narrated by Umm Salamah (may Allah be pleased with her)

Umm Salamah said that after her husband Abū Salamah died, she said it, and Allah later replaced him with the Messenger of Allah ﷺ.

Sahih Muslim 918Ṣaḥīḥ

The Prophet ﷺ passed by a woman weeping at a grave and told her to fear Allah and be patient. She did not recognise him and said, “Leave me, you have not been struck by my calamity.” When she learned who he was, she came to him and said she hadn’t known. He said: “Patience is at the first stroke of the calamity.”

Narrated by Anas ibn Mālik (may Allah be pleased with him)

Patience is being steady in the first shock; it is not a promise that you won’t cry.

Sahih al-Bukhari 1283 Sahih Muslim 926Ṣaḥīḥ

A man said to the Prophet ﷺ: “My mother died suddenly and I think that if she could speak she would have given charity. If I give charity on her behalf, will she receive the reward?” He said: “Yes.”

Narrated by ʿĀʾishah (may Allah be pleased with her)

A way to keep loving the one you lost.

Sahih al-Bukhari 1388 Sahih Muslim 1004Ṣaḥīḥ

“When a person dies, his deeds come to an end except for three: ongoing charity, knowledge from which people benefit, or a righteous child who prays for him.”

Narrated by Abū Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him)

Your prayers for them are a gift that still reaches them.

Sahih Muslim 1631Ṣaḥīḥ

“No fatigue, illness, worry, grief, harm or distress afflicts a Muslim – even the prick of a thorn – except that Allah expiates some of his sins through it.”

Narrated by Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī and Abū Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with them)

Sahih al-Bukhari 5641 Sahih Muslim 2573Ṣaḥīḥ

The du’a of distress

لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ الْعَظِيمُ الْحَلِيمُ، لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ رَبُّ الْعَرْشِ الْعَظِيمِ، لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ رَبُّ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَرَبُّ الْأَرْضِ وَرَبُّ الْعَرْشِ الْكَرِيمِ

Lā ilāha illallāhul-ʿAẓīmul-Ḥalīm, lā ilāha illallāhu Rabbul-ʿarshil-ʿaẓīm, lā ilāha illallāhu Rabbus-samāwāti wa Rabbul-arḍi wa Rabbul-ʿarshil-karīm.

There is no god but Allah, the Mighty, the Forbearing. There is no god but Allah, Lord of the Mighty Throne. There is no god but Allah, Lord of the heavens, Lord of the earth and Lord of the Noble Throne.

Reported by Ibn ʿAbbās (may Allah be pleased with them both): the Prophet ﷺ used to say this at times of distress.

Sahih al-Bukhari 6346 Sahih Muslim 2730Ṣaḥīḥ
❋

Gentle, practical steps

Small things you can try today. Not religious rulings, just ordinary wellbeing help that sits well alongside the guidance above.

  1. Let yourself cry. Hold back nothing that is simply sorrow.
  2. Say their name. Tell a story about them to someone who knew them.
  3. Give charity or pray for them (see the hadith above) as a way of continuing to love them.
  4. Keep tiny routines: water, food, a walk, sleep. Grief takes a lot of energy.
  5. If grief stays as heavy and constant after many weeks, ask a doctor or counsellor for help.
