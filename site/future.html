Fear of the Future

Jobs, AI, the economy, relationships, housing

No generation has had so many unknowns thrown at it at once. The Qur’an doesn’t promise a predictable future; it promises that the One who holds the future is Merciful, and it gives you a way to act without being consumed by fear.

On jobs, AI and a changing world

The Qur’an and Sunnah do not forecast job markets, but they do give a pattern: learn (20:114), work honestly with your own hands (Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 2072), make a plan, and rely on Allah (3:159). Skills change; Allah as your Provider does not.

To feel better: do this

Don’t try everything. Pick one that feels doable, and do it now. Fixed numbers are given only where the hadith gives them; otherwise say it as often as you like.

Lā ḥawla wa lā quwwata illā billāh

Say it whenever you feel you don’t have the strength. It means admitting that your strength comes from Allah.

لَا حَوْلَ وَلَا قُوَّةَ إِلَّا بِاللَّهِ

Lā ḥawla wa lā quwwata illā billāh.

There is no power and no strength except with Allah.

“Shall I not tell you of a treasure from the treasures of Paradise? It is: lā ḥawla wa lā quwwata illā billāh.” (Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī (may Allah be pleased with him))

Sahih al-Bukhari 6384 Sahih Muslim 2704

Pour your heart out in sujūd

Pray two rakʿahs, or your next prayer, and make a lot of du’a in prostration. Tell Allah everything that is on your heart.

The closest a servant is to his Lord is when he is prostrating, so make abundant du’a. (Abū Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him))

Sahih Muslim 482

Ask forgiveness: istighfār

Say it slowly, with your heart present. The Prophet ﷺ said he did this more than seventy times a day. No fixed number is required of you, so the counter is just a help.

أَسْتَغْفِرُ اللَّهَ وَأَتُوبُ إِلَيْهِ

Astaghfirullāha wa atūbu ilayh.

I seek forgiveness from Allah and turn to Him in repentance.

“By Allah, I seek forgiveness from Allah and turn to Him in repentance more than seventy times a day.” (Abū Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him))

Sahih al-Bukhari 6307

The three Quls, morning and evening

Recite Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ, al-Falaq and an-Nās, three times each, in the morning and in the evening.

“Allah is enough for us”

حَسْبُنَا اللَّهُ وَنِعْمَ الْوَكِيلُ

Ḥasbunallāhu wa niʿmal-wakīl.

Allah is sufficient for us, and He is the best disposer of affairs.

Ibn ʿAbbās reported that Ibrāhīm said it when he was thrown into the fire, and Muḥammad ﷺ said it when people warned: “The people have gathered against you.”

Qur’an 3:173 Sahih al-Bukhari 4563Ṣaḥīḥ

When you catch yourself saying “if only…”

قَدَرُ اللَّهِ وَمَا شَاءَ فَعَلَ

Qadarullāhi wa mā shāʾa faʿal.

Allah has decreed, and what He willed He did.

The Prophet ﷺ taught to say this instead of “if only I had…”, because “if only” opens the door to the whispers of Satan.

Sahih Muslim 2664Ṣaḥīḥ

Gentle, practical steps

Small things you can try today. Not religious rulings, just ordinary wellbeing help that sits well alongside the guidance above.

  1. Separate today’s worries into “can act on” and “can’t control”. Act on the first list; make du’a over the second.
  2. Build one transferable skill per quarter. You’re not behind; you’re compounding.
  3. Talk to people a few steps ahead of you in your field about what’s actually changing.
  4. Pray istikhārah for big decisions, then move.
