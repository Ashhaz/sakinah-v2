Heart & mind

Overwhelmed by News & Online Arguments

Disasters, conflict and comment-section wars, all day

You may care deeply, and that is a good thing, but a heart not built to carry the whole world’s suffering every minute will eventually crack. Here is how the Qur’an and Sunnah guide care, restraint and rest.

Caring without collapsing

The Prophet ﷺ described believers as one body who feel each other’s pain (Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 6011). That care is real, but so is your capacity: “Allah does not charge a soul except [with that within] its capacity” (2:286). Give what you can, make du’a, then step back and rest.

To feel better: do this

Don’t try everything. Pick one that feels doable, and do it now. Fixed numbers are given only where the hadith gives them; otherwise say it as often as you like.

Lā ḥawla wa lā quwwata illā billāh

Say it whenever you feel you don’t have the strength. It means admitting that your strength comes from Allah.

لَا حَوْلَ وَلَا قُوَّةَ إِلَّا بِاللَّهِ

Lā ḥawla wa lā quwwata illā billāh.

There is no power and no strength except with Allah.

“Shall I not tell you of a treasure from the treasures of Paradise? It is: lā ḥawla wa lā quwwata illā billāh.” (Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī (may Allah be pleased with him))

Sahih al-Bukhari 6384 Sahih Muslim 2704

Remember Allah with the four beloved phrases

Breathe slowly and say any of these quietly, as many times as feels right. There is no fixed number, so the counters are only a help.

سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ، وَالْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ، وَلَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ، وَاللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ

SubḥānAllāh, wal-ḥamdu lillāh, wa lā ilāha illallāh, wallāhu akbar.

Glory be to Allah; praise be to Allah; there is no god but Allah; Allah is the Greatest.

“The most beloved words to Allah are four: SubḥānAllāh, Alḥamdulillāh, Lā ilāha illallāh and Allāhu akbar. It does not matter which one you begin with.” (Samurah ibn Jundub (may Allah be pleased with him))

Also: “Truly, in the remembrance of Allah do hearts find peace” (13:28), and the Prophet ﷺ said the one who remembers his Lord and the one who does not are like the living and the dead (Bukhari 6407).

The du’a of distress

لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ الْعَظِيمُ الْحَلِيمُ، لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ رَبُّ الْعَرْشِ الْعَظِيمِ، لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ رَبُّ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَرَبُّ الْأَرْضِ وَرَبُّ الْعَرْشِ الْكَرِيمِ

Lā ilāha illallāhul-ʿAẓīmul-Ḥalīm, lā ilāha illallāhu Rabbul-ʿarshil-ʿaẓīm, lā ilāha illallāhu Rabbus-samāwāti wa Rabbul-arḍi wa Rabbul-ʿarshil-karīm.

There is no god but Allah, the Mighty, the Forbearing. There is no god but Allah, Lord of the Mighty Throne. There is no god but Allah, Lord of the heavens, Lord of the earth and Lord of the Noble Throne.

Reported by Ibn ʿAbbās (may Allah be pleased with them both): the Prophet ﷺ used to say this at times of distress.

Sahih al-Bukhari 6346 Sahih Muslim 2730Ṣaḥīḥ

Gentle, practical steps

Small things you can try today. Not religious rulings, just ordinary wellbeing help that sits well alongside the guidance above.

  1. Pick two fixed times a day to check news. Outside those times, leave it.
  2. Convert helplessness into action: donate, volunteer, or make a specific du’a for the people you saw.
  3. Don’t argue with strangers. Mute, block, move on.
  4. Make wuḍū’ when you’re worked up. It is a physical pause in your day.
