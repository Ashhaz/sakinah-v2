Family Pressure & Conflict at Home

Expectations, arguments and feeling unheard by the people closest to you

Islam puts great weight on kindness to parents and family ties, and also acknowledges that family life isn’t always easy. This page holds both.

If you are being hurt at home

Being kind to parents does not mean staying silent while being abused or in danger. If you are being hurt, physically or emotionally, please tell a trusted adult, a teacher or a local helpline, and keep yourself safe.

To feel better: do this

Don’t try everything. Pick one that feels doable, and do it now. Fixed numbers are given only where the hadith gives them; otherwise say it as often as you like.

Pour your heart out in sujūd

Pray two rakʿahs, or your next prayer, and make a lot of du’a in prostration. Tell Allah everything that is on your heart.

The closest a servant is to his Lord is when he is prostrating, so make abundant du’a. (Abū Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him))

Sahih Muslim 482

Do one small kindness today

Send a kind message, smile, check on someone, or help with something small. Doing good for others often lifts the heart.

“Do not belittle any good deed, even if it is meeting your brother with a cheerful face.” (Abū Dharr (may Allah be pleased with him))

And: Allah helps His servant as long as the servant is helping his brother (Muslim 2699).

Sahih Muslim 2626 Sahih Muslim 2699

Remember Allah with the four beloved phrases

Breathe slowly and say any of these quietly, as many times as feels right. There is no fixed number, so the counters are only a help.

سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ، وَالْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ، وَلَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ، وَاللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ

SubḥānAllāh, wal-ḥamdu lillāh, wa lā ilāha illallāh, wallāhu akbar.

Glory be to Allah; praise be to Allah; there is no god but Allah; Allah is the Greatest.

“The most beloved words to Allah are four: SubḥānAllāh, Alḥamdulillāh, Lā ilāha illallāh and Allāhu akbar. It does not matter which one you begin with.” (Samurah ibn Jundub (may Allah be pleased with him))

Gentle, practical steps

Small things you can try today. Not religious rulings, just ordinary wellbeing help that sits well alongside the guidance above.

  1. Pick a calm moment (not in the middle of an argument) and start with: “I want us to understand each other.”
  2. Listen first. Repeat back what you heard before you explain yourself.
  3. State one clear, respectful boundary rather than a long list of complaints.
  4. If it is stuck, bring in a trusted relative, teacher or imam to help.
  5. Make du’a for them, even while you are upset. It softens your heart too.
