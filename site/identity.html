Faith & the soul

Identity Crisis: “Who Am I?”

Pulled between family, culture, faith, friends and the internet

Many young people feel like different people in different rooms. The Qur’an gives a steady answer to “who am I?”: a created, honoured human being, with a purpose, accountable to the One who made you.

Questions are not a crisis of faith

Wondering who you are, what you believe and where you belong is a normal part of growing up. Ask your questions of someone knowledgeable, kind and patient. If this feeling comes with deep distress, see the pages on depression and hopelessness.

To feel better: do this

Don’t try everything. Pick one that feels doable, and do it now. Fixed numbers are given only where the hadith gives them; otherwise say it as often as you like.

Read a few verses slowly

Open the Qur’an, read a short passage with a translation, and let it be a conversation. Even five verses count.

…

O mankind, there has come to you instruction from your Lord and healing for what is in the breasts and guidance and mercy for the believers.

“Recite the Qur’an, for it will come on the Day of Resurrection as an intercessor for those who recite it.” (Abū Umāmah al-Bāhilī (may Allah be pleased with him))

Qur’an 10:57 Sahih Muslim 804

Pour your heart out in sujūd

Pray two rakʿahs, or your next prayer, and make a lot of du’a in prostration. Tell Allah everything that is on your heart.

The closest a servant is to his Lord is when he is prostrating, so make abundant du’a. (Abū Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him))

Sahih Muslim 482

Remember Allah with the four beloved phrases

Breathe slowly and say any of these quietly, as many times as feels right. There is no fixed number, so the counters are only a help.

سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ، وَالْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ، وَلَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ، وَاللَّهُ أَكْبَرُ

SubḥānAllāh, wal-ḥamdu lillāh, wa lā ilāha illallāh, wallāhu akbar.

Glory be to Allah; praise be to Allah; there is no god but Allah; Allah is the Greatest.

“The most beloved words to Allah are four: SubḥānAllāh, Alḥamdulillāh, Lā ilāha illallāh and Allāhu akbar. It does not matter which one you begin with.” (Samurah ibn Jundub (may Allah be pleased with him))

Also: “Truly, in the remembrance of Allah do hearts find peace” (13:28), and the Prophet ﷺ said the one who remembers his Lord and the one who does not are like the living and the dead (Bukhari 6407).

Sahih Muslim 2137 Qur’an 13:28 Sahih al-Bukhari 6407

Send blessings upon the Prophet ﷺ

Say “ṣallallāhu ʿalayhi wa sallam” or the fuller ṣalawāt of the prayer. It is also a way to feel close to him.

…

Indeed, Allah and His angels send blessings upon the Prophet. O you who have believed, ask [Allah to confer] blessing upon him and ask [Allah to grant him] peace.

“Whoever sends blessings upon me once, Allah will send blessings upon him ten times.” (Abū Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him))

Ubayy ibn Kaʿb (may Allah be pleased with him) asked the Prophet ﷺ how much of his du’a he should devote to sending blessings on him. When Ubayy offered all of it, the Prophet ﷺ said: “Then your worries will be taken care of and your sins forgiven” (Tirmidhī 2457, graded ḥasan).

Gentle, practical steps

Small things you can try today. Not religious rulings, just ordinary wellbeing help that sits well alongside the guidance above.

  1. Write your three most important values (for example honesty, kindness, learning). Check one decision this week against them.
  2. List the roles you hold (child, student, friend, believer). Notice which ones you’re neglecting and which you’re hiding.
  3. Find one person you respect who is both thoughtful and kind, and ask them how they found their way.
  4. Be the same person in every room, even if you’re quieter in some.
  5. Pray, even briefly. It reminds you daily who you’re answerable to and who you belong to.
