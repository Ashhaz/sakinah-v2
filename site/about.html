About & sources

What this is, how to check it, and what it is not.

Why Sakīna exists

Sakīna (سكينة) means tranquility, the calm Allah places in hearts. This site gathers Qur’an and Hadith references on problems many young people face today: stress, loneliness, comparison, burnout, sleep, screens and more. The aim is comfort in dark times.

How references are written

  • Qur’an: shown as surah:verse (for example 94:5–6), each linking to Quran.com. The Arabic text is fetched live from the Qur’an API so that no letter is typed by hand. English is a translation of the meaning, not the Qur’an itself.
  • Hadith: shown as collection + number (for example Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 5641), each linking to Sunnah.com. Numbering follows Sunnah.com; some editions number hadith differently, so if a number looks off in your printed copy, search by the wording.
  • Grading: hadith found in Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī or Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim are marked Ṣaḥīḥ. Others carry the grade given by scholars (for example Ḥasan). Weak hadith are not used.
  • Partial verses: where only part of a verse is quoted, the card says so and sends you to the full verse.
  • Interpretations: the short notes under each reference are reflections, not tafsīr or fatwā.

Review status

This is a personal project and has not yet been formally reviewed by a qualified scholar or a mental health professional. Please treat it as a work in progress. The references page lists every verse and hadith so a reviewer can check them.

Please verify and ask

Please check anything important yourself on Quran.com and Sunnah.com, and ask a qualified scholar about religious rulings. If you find a mistake, please report it so that it can be corrected.

What this site is not

It is not medical or psychological advice and it is not a substitute for therapy, medication or professional care. Depression, anxiety and other conditions are real health issues. Seeking treatment is encouraged in the Sunnah: “Allah has not sent down a disease except that He has also sent down its cure” (Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 5678).

If it feels like too much right now

If you are thinking about ending your life or harming yourself, or you are in danger, please contact your local emergency number or a crisis line now, and tell someone near you. You deserve support, and reaching out is a strength.

  • India: Tele-MANAS, 14416 (toll-free; also 1800-891-4416)
  • USA: call or text 988
  • UK & Ireland: Samaritans, 116 123
  • Anywhere: findahelpline.com lists services by country

Numbers can change, so please check one is current for your country. Allah says: “…and do not kill yourselves. Indeed, Allah is ever Merciful to you” (Qur’an 4:29, part of the verse). You can also speak to a trusted imam, teacher or family member, and to a doctor or counsellor.
