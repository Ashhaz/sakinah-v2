Hopelessness: “What’s the Point?”

When you can’t see a way forward and nothing feels like it will change

Hopelessness is one of the heaviest feelings there is, and the Qur’an speaks to it directly. Even prophets said, “When will Allah’s help come?” and the answer was: it is near.

Hopelessness is a symptom, not a verdict

Feeling that nothing will ever improve is a common sign of depression, and it lies. It can make the future look closed when it isn’t. Please speak to a doctor or counsellor, and to someone you trust. The Prophet ﷺ taught that every disease has a cure (Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 5678).

To feel better: do this

Don’t try everything. Pick one that feels doable, and do it now. Fixed numbers are given only where the hadith gives them; otherwise say it as often as you like.

Raise your hands and ask

Raise your hands and ask Allah for what you need, in your own words. A single sentence is enough.

“Your Lord is Ever-Living and Generous. He is shy to turn away empty the hands of His servant when he raises them to Him.” (Salmān al-Fārisī (may Allah be pleased with him))

Sunan Abi Dawud 1488 Jami‘ at-Tirmidhi 3556Ḥasan (Tirmidhī); Ṣaḥīḥ (al-Albānī)

Pour your heart out in sujūd

Pray two rakʿahs, or your next prayer, and make a lot of du’a in prostration. Tell Allah everything that is on your heart.

The closest a servant is to his Lord is when he is prostrating, so make abundant du’a. (Abū Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him))

Sahih Muslim 482

Read a few verses slowly

Open the Qur’an, read a short passage with a translation, and let it be a conversation. Even five verses count.

…

O mankind, there has come to you instruction from your Lord and healing for what is in the breasts and guidance and mercy for the believers.

“Recite the Qur’an, for it will come on the Day of Resurrection as an intercessor for those who recite it.” (Abū Umāmah al-Bāhilī (may Allah be pleased with him))

Qur’an 10:57 Sahih Muslim 804

Do one small kindness today

Send a kind message, smile, check on someone, or help with something small. Doing good for others often lifts the heart.

“Do not belittle any good deed, even if it is meeting your brother with a cheerful face.” (Abū Dharr (may Allah be pleased with him))

And: Allah helps His servant as long as the servant is helping his brother (Muslim 2699).

The du’a for sorrow and anxiety

اللَّهُمَّ إِنِّي عَبْدُكَ، ابْنُ عَبْدِكَ، ابْنُ أَمَتِكَ، نَاصِيَتِي بِيَدِكَ، مَاضٍ فِيَّ حُكْمُكَ، عَدْلٌ فِيَّ قَضَاؤُكَ، أَسْأَلُكَ بِكُلِّ اسْمٍ هُوَ لَكَ سَمَّيْتَ بِهِ نَفْسَكَ، أَوْ عَلَّمْتَهُ أَحَدًا مِنْ خَلْقِكَ، أَوْ أَنْزَلْتَهُ فِي كِتَابِكَ، أَوِ اسْتَأْثَرْتَ بِهِ فِي عِلْمِ الْغَيْبِ عِنْدَكَ، أَنْ تَجْعَلَ الْقُرْآنَ رَبِيعَ قَلْبِي، وَنُورَ صَدْرِي، وَجَلَاءَ حُزْنِي، وَذَهَابَ هَمِّي

Allāhumma innī ʿabduka, ibnu ʿabdika, ibnu amatik. Nāṣiyatī biyadik, māḍin fiyya ḥukmuk, ʿadlun fiyya qaḍāʾuk. Asʾaluka bikulli ismin huwa lak, sammayta bihi nafsak, aw ʿallamtahu aḥadan min khalqik, aw anzaltahu fī kitābik, awistaʾtharta bihi fī ʿilmil-ghaybi ʿindak, an tajʿalal-Qurʾāna rabīʿa qalbī, wa nūra ṣadrī, wa jalāʾa ḥuznī, wa dhahāba hammī.

O Allah, I am Your servant, the son of Your servant, the son of Your maidservant. My forelock is in Your hand; Your command over me is carried out; Your decree concerning me is just. I ask You by every name that is Yours — by which You named Yourself, or taught to any of Your creation, or revealed in Your Book, or kept to Yourself in the knowledge of the unseen — to make the Qur’an the spring of my heart, the light of my chest, the departure of my sorrow and the release of my worry.

The Prophet ﷺ said that whoever is afflicted by worry or grief and says this, Allah will remove his worry and grief and replace it with relief. The Companions asked if they should learn it; he said yes, whoever hears it should learn it.

Musnad Aḥmad (ḥadīth of Ibn Masʿūd) – graded Ṣaḥīḥ by Ibn Ḥibbān and al-Albānī, Silsilah aṣ-Ṣaḥīḥah no. 199

The du’a of Yūnus from the darkness

لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا أَنْتَ سُبْحَانَكَ إِنِّي كُنْتُ مِنَ الظَّالِمِينَ

Lā ilāha illā anta, subḥānaka, innī kuntu minaẓ-ẓālimīn.

There is no god but You; glory be to You. Truly, I have been among the wrongdoers.

The Prophet ﷺ said that no Muslim supplicates his Lord with these words for anything, except that Allah answers him (Tirmidhī 3505, from Saʿd ibn Abī Waqqāṣ).

Qur’an 21:87 Jami‘ at-Tirmidhi 3505Ṣaḥīḥ (al-Albānī)

Gentle, practical steps

Small things you can try today. Not religious rulings, just ordinary wellbeing help that sits well alongside the guidance above.

  1. Do one tiny thing today: a shower, a glass of water, a short walk. Hope often returns after small actions.
  2. Tell one person the truth in a sentence: “I feel hopeless.” You don’t have to explain it.
  3. Don’t decide anything big while you feel this way. The feeling distorts how the future looks.
  4. See a doctor or counsellor. This is treatable, and asking is not a lack of faith.
  5. Read the accounts of Yaʿqūb and Yūsuf ﷺ (surah 12). A man who lost hope for years was reunited with his son.

If it feels like too much right now

If you are thinking about ending your life or harming yourself, or you are in danger, please contact your local emergency number or a crisis line now, and tell someone near you. You deserve support, and reaching out is a strength.

  • India: Tele-MANAS, 14416 (toll-free; also 1800-891-4416)
  • USA: call or text 988
  • UK & Ireland: Samaritans, 116 123
  • Anywhere: findahelpline.com lists services by country

Numbers can change, so please check one is current for your country. Allah says: “…and do not kill yourselves. Indeed, Allah is ever Merciful to you” (Qur’an 4:29, part of the verse). You can also speak to a trusted imam, teacher or family member, and to a doctor or counsellor.
