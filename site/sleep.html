Sleep Problems

Too late, too little, too much phone

Sleep is not wasted time in Islam. It is mentioned as a mercy, a sign and a design. The Sunnah also gives a bedtime routine that many people find calming.

Take a breath first

Slow breathing helps your body calm down. Tap the circle, breathe with it, and if you like, remember Allah in your heart as you do (13:28).

To feel better: do this

Don’t try everything. Pick one that feels doable, and do it now. Fixed numbers are given only where the hadith gives them; otherwise say it as often as you like.

Ayat al-Kursī before sleep

Read Ayat al-Kursī when you go to bed.

…

Allah – there is no deity except Him, the Ever-Living, the Sustainer of [all] existence. Neither drowsiness overtakes Him nor sleep. To Him belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. Who is it that can intercede with Him except by His permission? He knows what is [presently] before them and what will be after them, and they encompass not a thing of His knowledge except for what He wills. His Kursī extends over the heavens and the earth, and their preservation tires Him not. And He is the Most High, the Most Great.

“When you go to your bed, recite Āyat al-Kursī; a guardian from Allah will stay with you and Satan will not come near you until morning.” (Abū Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him))

Qur’an 2:255 Sahih al-Bukhari 2311

The three Quls, morning and evening

Recite Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ, al-Falaq and an-Nās, three times each, in the morning and in the evening.

…

Say, “He is Allah, [who is] One, Allah, the Eternal Refuge. He neither begets nor is born, nor is there to Him any equivalent.” Say, “I seek refuge in the Lord of daybreak, from the evil of that which He created, and from the evil of darkness when it settles, and from the evil of the blowers in knots, and from the evil of an envier when he envies.” Say, “I seek refuge in the Lord of mankind, the Sovereign of mankind, the God of mankind, from the evil of the retreating whisperer – who whispers [evil] into the breasts of mankind – among jinn and among mankind.”

“Recite “Qul huwa Allāhu aḥad” and the two Muʿawwidhatayn three times in the morning and in the evening; they will suffice you against everything.” (ʿAbdullāh ibn Khubayb (may Allah be pleased with him))

Qur’an 112:1–4 Qur’an 113:1–5 Qur’an 114:1–6 Sunan Abi Dawud 5082 Jami‘ at-Tirmidhi 3575Ḥasan Ṣaḥīḥ (Tirmidhī)

The bedtime tasbīḥ

When you lie down, say SubḥānAllāh 33 times, Alḥamdulillāh 33 times and Allāhu akbar 34 times.

“When you go to your bed, say SubḥānAllāh thirty-three times, Alḥamdulillāh thirty-three times and Allāhu akbar thirty-four times; that is better for you than a servant.” (ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib (may Allah be pleased with him))

Sahih al-Bukhari 3113 Sahih Muslim 2727

II

From the Prophet ﷺ

“The Prophet ﷺ disliked sleeping before the ʿIshāʾ prayer and talking after it.”

Narrated by Abū Barzah al-Aslamī (may Allah be pleased with him)

Scholars explain the “talking” as idle conversation that delays sleep and the pre-dawn prayer; beneficial talk (like study or family matters) is treated differently.

Sahih al-Bukhari 568 Sahih Muslim 647Ṣaḥīḥ

The Prophet ﷺ said: “When you go to your bed, perform wuḍūʾ as for prayer, then lie on your right side and say: O Allah, I submit myself to You, I entrust my affairs to You, I turn my face to You and I rely on You, out of hope and fear of You. There is no refuge and no escape from You except to You. I believe in Your Book which You revealed and Your Prophet whom You sent.” And he said: “If you die that night, you will die upon the fiṭrah, and make these the last words you say.”

Narrated by al-Barāʾ ibn ʿĀzib (may Allah be pleased with him)

Sahih al-Bukhari 247 Sahih Muslim 2710Ṣaḥīḥ

Whenever the Prophet ﷺ went to his bed each night, he would cup his hands together, blow into them and recite into them Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ, Sūrat al-Falaq and Sūrat an-Nās, then wipe with them over as much of his body as he could, starting with his head, face and the front of his body. He would do that three times.

Narrated by ʿĀʾishah (may Allah be pleased with her)

Sahih al-Bukhari 5017Ṣaḥīḥ

“The one who recites Āyat al-Kursī when going to bed will have a guardian from Allah over him, and Satan will not come near him until morning.”

Narrated by Abū Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him)

Abū Hurayrah was guarding charity dates when someone came and taught him this. The Prophet ﷺ confirmed it, saying: “He told you the truth, though he is a liar.”

Sahih al-Bukhari 2311Ṣaḥīḥ

“Whoever recites the last two verses of Sūrat al-Baqarah at night, they will suffice him.”

Narrated by Abū Masʿūd al-Badrī (may Allah be pleased with him)

Scholars mention several meanings of “suffice”, including protection and counting as sufficient night worship.

Sahih al-Bukhari 5009 Sahih Muslim 807Ṣaḥīḥ

The Prophet ﷺ said: “If one of you becomes drowsy while praying, let him sleep until his drowsiness is gone, for if one of you prays while drowsy he may not know whether he is asking forgiveness or insulting himself.”

Narrated by ʿĀʾishah (may Allah be pleased with her)

The Prophet ﷺ himself put sleep above forcing yourself through worship while exhausted.

Sahih al-Bukhari 212Ṣaḥīḥ

Allah descends to the lowest heaven in the last third of every night and says: “Who is calling upon Me that I may answer him? Who is asking of Me that I may give him? Who is seeking My forgiveness that I may forgive him?”

Narrated by Abū Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him)

If you’re awake in the dark hours anyway, here’s a way to use the time.

Narrated by ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAmr (may Allah be pleased with him)

Gentle, practical steps

Small things you can try today. Not religious rulings, just ordinary wellbeing help that sits well alongside the guidance above.

  1. Pick a fixed wake-up time, including weekends. Your body clock loves a pattern.
  2. Put the phone on a charger across the room 45–60 minutes before bed.
  3. Dim lights, avoid caffeine in the late afternoon, and don’t eat a heavy meal right before sleep.
  4. Try the bedtime routine of the Sunnah: wuḍūʾ, Āyat al-Kursī, the last two verses of al-Baqarah, the three Quls, then the du’a.
  5. If insomnia lasts for weeks, talk to a doctor. Persistent sleep problems have treatable causes.
